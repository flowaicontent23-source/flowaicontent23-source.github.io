## The Photograph on the Wall

Every year, on the same October night, the photograph on my grandmother's wall changed.

A family portrait from nineteen fifty-two, eight people on a porch.

I counted them every visit. The count was always the same.

## There Were Nine

Then last October, there were nine.

A man stood at the far end, half hidden in shadow.

No one recognised him. His face was turned away.

My grandmother went pale. The photo had never been touched.

## Back on the Wall

That night, I locked it in a drawer.

By morning, it was back on the wall. The man had moved closer.

Now he stood second from the left, beside my great-grandfather.

## The Letter in the Attic

I searched the attic for other copies. I found only a letter.

Dated nineteen fifty-three, never sent. It said he is coming back.

One step at a time, until he is inside the house.

I burned the letter, but the photograph would not burn.

---

This October, the man was standing in the centre, reaching toward the camera.

Tonight is October again. If you have an old photograph, count the people twice.
