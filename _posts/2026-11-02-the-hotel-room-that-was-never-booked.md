## Past Midnight

It was past midnight when I reached the hotel, tired, with only one bag.

I had a booking. I had the confirmation on my phone. That should have been the end of it — a key, a room, a bed, sleep.

It was not.

## The Room That Was Never Booked

The receptionist looked at my booking and went pale.

"Sir," she said, "this room was never booked."

I laughed. I showed her the confirmation on my phone. She looked at it, then at her own screen, and said it again, quieter this time, as if saying it softly would make it less impossible.

"Sir, this hotel has no room with that number."

Then she gave me the key anyway.

Room 404. Fourth floor. End of the corridor.

## The Corridor Was Too Long

The corridor was too long.

Every door was closed, except one. Mine was already open.

I had not opened it. I had not even reached it yet when I saw the light spilling out from under the gap. A room that did not exist, at the end of a corridor that went on too far, was waiting for me with its door open.

Inside, the bed was made. The light was on.

And on the table, there was a suitcase. With my name on it.

I had not unpacked. I had not even entered the room before.

## You Checked In Years Ago

Then the phone rang.

A voice, calm, like my own, said: "You checked in years ago. You just forgot to leave."

I ran to the reception. The desk was empty. The register was open. My name was not in it.

But on the last page, in my own handwriting, it said: "Do not check out. You are already home."
